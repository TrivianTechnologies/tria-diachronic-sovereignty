# Example: Repair Without Restoration

A persistent assistant acted on an outdated scheduling preference after the participant had already narrowed that preference.

The participant records a rupture:

- affected scope: scheduling and personalization;
- expectation: current explicit instruction should supersede stale inference;
- impact: unwanted scheduling action and reduced trust;
- status: acknowledged.

The system offers:

1. correct the scheduling state;
2. mark the stale inference as non-operative;
3. require re-consent before future persistent scheduling;
4. review the provenance path that allowed stale state to remain operative.

The participant accepts 1–3 and holds 4 for later review.

The outcome records:

**Restored**
- current scheduling state;
- explicit consent boundary.

**Remains changed**
- trust is not represented as returned to its prior state;
- future scheduling authority is narrower.

**Unresolved**
- whether additional retained context should be released.

The relationship continues under a new baseline.

This is considered a repair even though the pre-rupture relationship was not recreated.
