# Rupture and Repair

**Epistemic status: NORMATIVE / SPECIFICATION PRECURSOR**

Rupture is a material relational discontinuity in which an expectation, boundary, commitment, consent condition, authority condition, or trust-relevant pattern is asserted to have been violated, failed, or changed.

Repair is a governed response to rupture.

Repair is **not** synonymous with restoration, forgiveness, reconciliation, renewed consent, or continuation.

## Core invariant

> **A repair record must preserve what was restored, what remains changed, and what remains unresolved.**

A system must not use the existence of a repair attempt to imply that the prior relational state has been recovered.

## Rupture record

A rupture record should make legible:

- the rupture identifier;
- who recorded it;
- the affected scope;
- the expectation or boundary involved;
- the observed impact;
- evidence or source references where available;
- whether the characterization is acknowledged, contested, or unresolved;
- timestamp and provenance.

Recording a rupture does not establish blame, intent, or objective truth.

Different participants may hold non-equivalent accounts.

## Repair offer

A repair offer should state:

- which rupture it addresses;
- who is making the offer;
- which actions are offered;
- any limits or conditions;
- what the offer does **not** claim to restore.

An offer does not create an obligation to accept.

## Response

A repair response may be:

- accepted;
- partially accepted;
- declined;
- held for later review;
- or unresolved.

Declining repair must not be treated as hostility, consent to continued engagement, or waiver of exit rights.

## Outcome

A repair outcome should separately record:

- what was restored;
- what remains changed;
- what remains unresolved;
- whether permissions, authority, consent, or lifecycle state changed;
- any new baseline;
- any future review condition.

A successful repair may result in a relationship that is materially different from the pre-rupture relationship.

## Repair and re-baselining

Re-baselining and repair are related but non-equivalent.

A new baseline may be established:

- after repair;
- during partial repair;
- without repair;
- after a participant declines repair;
- as part of transformation or dissolution.

A new baseline is not proof that repair occurred.

## Repair and consent

Repair MUST NOT silently renew consent.

Any new or continued authorization required after rupture must follow the relevant consent and re-consent mechanisms.

## Repair and dissolution

Repair is not obligatory continuation.

Where applicable, legitimate outcomes include:

- narrowed engagement;
- dormancy;
- transformation;
- dissolution;
- scoped forgetting;
- or no further relationship.

## Provenance

Rupture and repair records should be append-only governance events or otherwise preserve attributable history.

Correction of a rupture record should create new provenance rather than silently rewriting the original account.

## Design principle

> **What was broken should not be erased by the story of repair. What was repaired should not be denied because change remains.**
